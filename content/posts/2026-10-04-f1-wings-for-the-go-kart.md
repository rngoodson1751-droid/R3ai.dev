I'm rebuilding a two-seat Thunder Kart for River. He loves Formula 1 and he loves Red Bull, so a plain go-kart was never going to be enough. It needs wings.

## The rules I set

- Cheap. I already had quarter-inch plywood and half-inch PVC pipe.
- No welding and no drilling into the frame. Everything clamps on with U-bolt conduit clamps and comes off again.
- Room for my knees. It's a two-seater and I ride along, so the front wing has to stay open on the sides.

## Front wing

A flat center section 18 inches wide sits over the front axle. Two outer sections, 8 inches each, angle down about 15 degrees, with tapered end plates standing up at the tips. The open sides that clear my knees end up making it look more like a real F1 nose, not less.

## Rear wing

This is the showpiece. It has two elements, like the real thing: a main plane 22 inches wide and a smaller flap about an inch and a half above it. They sit on two PVC uprights that lean forward like swan necks, between tall end plates that taper from back to front.

## Sidepods

Flat plywood panels along each side with a scoop shape at the front. They do nothing for airflow. They do a great deal for looking fast while parked.

## The finish

Everything gets sanded, then finished in matte black paint and carbon fiber heat shrink wrap. A heat gun pulls the wrap tight, and from a few feet away it reads as carbon fiber.

## The bill

About $50 in new parts: clamps, a little more pipe, fittings, brackets, sandpaper and two cans of paint. The plywood, the pipe and the wrap were already in the garage.

## Does any of this make it faster

No. At go-kart speeds a plywood wing is a decoration. But a kid who sees his kart with a rear wing on it doesn't need a wind tunnel to know it's faster. I'll post pictures as the build comes together.
