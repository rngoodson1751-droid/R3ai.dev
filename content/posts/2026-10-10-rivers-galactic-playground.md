River's astrophysics phase is accelerating. I would call it exponential. Every day there are more questions about space and aliens.

Well. Space and alien games.

But it's a start.

It also makes River do his happy dance. That is the only review I need.

[Play River's Galactic Playground](/games/rivers-galactic-playground/)

## Where it came from

We already had a small space game by this name, made with a different AI tool. It had four worlds to wander around in and some stars to pick up.

On a Saturday I handed that file to Claude and asked for a rebuild. I wanted three things: shiny 3D graphics, a wireless Xbox controller, and room for three players so the whole family fits on one couch. I may also have said it should rival the latest Mario.

The first four worlds came back in about an hour. I said it was great and asked for four more that were even better. Those took about half an hour.

## What's in it

Eleven worlds with three stars in each, 33 in all. You start on the Starship Plaza and walk into a glowing gate to pick a world. Every world is open from the start.

- **Rainbow Planet.** Stepping stones, bouncy mushrooms and a moving cloud. The Grumble Bots are grumpy until you spin next to them.
- **Cosmic Sky Ride.** Fly a saucer through twelve star rings and beam up four lost baby aliens.
- **Moon Kart Raceway.** Three laps against three robot rivals. With more than one player the screen splits.
- **Secret Satellite.** Low gravity, a spiral climb up the solar panels to the big dish, and three towers to switch on.
- **Dino Volcano.** Ride water spouts up the mountain, or walk up a long-neck dinosaur's tail and ride his head like an elevator. The lava at the top is a trampoline. There are four eggs to hatch.
- **Snowball Comet.** Take a gondola to the summit and ride a long ice slide back down to a frozen lake. Touch a lost penguin and it follows you. Lead all four home to the igloo.
- **Comet Surf.** Hoverboards on a rainbow road that loops through space. Lean, jump off the launch pads and spin in the air for tricks.
- **Dusty Canyon Dash.** I asked for a desert canyon race in the spirit of a famous one from the movies. So there are hover racers with two giant rocket engines each, red rock walls and stone arches to fly under. This one is the whole family. River wears a golden robot costume. I am a small green sage with very long ears. Rachelle runs the junk shop by the start line and bolts a new part onto your racer after each race.
- **King Grumble's Castle.** A boss battle. Jump over his shockwave rings, wait until he gets tired, then run up and spin to tickle him. Three tickles and he turns green and smiles.
- **Mighty Makers HQ.** The one with homework hidden in it. More on that below.
- **Rainbow Bike Delivery.** The newest world. Rachelle rides a unicorn down a gloomy street and throws rainbows at the houses. Also below.

Find all 33 stars and the rocket on the plaza launches with fireworks.

## Update: a superhero headquarters

River is deep in superheroes now, alongside the astrophysics. So the plaza got a tenth gate, right behind where you start, and it goes to Mighty Makers HQ.

I asked for something in the spirit of the kid version of a certain famous superhero team. Claude said no to the actual characters, which are somebody else's, and made our own instead. I think ours are better. I am biased.

- **River** is the hero in charge. He wears a Mega Suit he built himself, with rocket boots and a cape.
- **Captain Gale** is me. A sailor captain in navy and yellow who runs on wind power. Make of that what you will.
- **Professor Prism** is Rachelle. Goggles, a lab cape and a prism that turns light into a rainbow.
- **Gus the Gem Giant** is the big one. He is made of purple crystal, he is very strong and he is very gentle.

Every mission is a little science lesson wearing a costume.

- **Spin three wind turbines.** Each one explains a piece of how wind becomes electricity. When all three spin, the wires light up in a loop to the charger, and the game says the word circuit out loud. River's Mega Suit powers up and everyone's rocket boots get a third jump.
- **Ride Gus's lever.** Stand on one end of a giant seesaw and Gus stomps on the other. You fly up to the Sky Lab. The voice explains that a lever is a simple machine. A bubble floats you back down.
- **Professor Prism's quiz.** Five questions picked from fourteen: magnets, floating, planets, gravity, rainbows, shapes, counting. The voice reads the question and the choices, and you answer by walking onto the right picture. A wrong answer greys out that pad and gives a hint. Nobody can lose, same as everywhere else.

{{photo /shots/galactic-mighty-makers.jpg Professor Prism's lab. Step on a color pad to answer.}}

The quiz is meant for a kindergartner who is learning to read in two languages. If the questions turn out too easy, that is a good problem and I will ask for harder ones.

## Update: Rainbow Bike Delivery

Paperboy is an old school arcade game. You ride a bike down a street and throw newspapers at the houses that ordered one. I asked Claude for that game, with one change: Rachelle rides a unicorn and delivers rainbows and sunshine instead of the morning paper.

{{photo /shots/galactic-rainbow-warrior.jpg Rachelle the Rainbow Warrior: a crown, a rainbow cape, a rainbow shield and a unicorn with golden hooves.}}

The street keeps rolling toward you, the way Paperboy's did. A thunderstorm named Grumbleboom has made half the houses grey, each with its own little rain cloud. Press the blue button to throw a rainbow to the left and the red button to throw to the right. Hit a grey house and it turns bright, a sun comes out, a rainbow arches over the roof and the family runs out to dance.

Grumbleboom floats ahead the whole way and drops lightning. A yellow ring on the road shows where it will land, so you can steer out of the way. Puddles make the unicorn stumble. Rainbow stripes across the road launch you into the air, and the rainbow gems float up there.

{{photo /shots/galactic-rainbow-delivery.jpg Halfway down the street. The bright houses got their rainbows. The grey ones are still waiting.}}

At the end of the street is a park where Grumbleboom comes down to fight. Five hits of sunshine turn him from dark grey to white, his frown turns upside down and a giant rainbow fills the sky. The game says he just needed some sunshine, and now he waters the flowers instead.

The stars: brighten 10 houses, cheer up the storm and find 5 rainbow gems. The houses stay bright between rides, so a slow first ride still counts.

Player one is always Rachelle in this world. If River and I join, River rides a blue unicorn in his Mega Suit and I ride a lavender one in my captain's hat. Nobody can lose here either. Lightning only knocks you sideways for a second.

## Three players, and two names I didn't ask for

Anyone can join at any time by pressing A on their own controller. Up to three people play on one screen.

The first hero is River. Claude named the other two Zib and Luma, after the friends in [the comic River wrote](/blog/a-father-son-comic-about-physics/). I didn't ask for that. It was the right call.

{{photo /shots/rivers-galactic-playground.jpg Comet Surf with all three heroes: Zib, River and Luma.}}

Player one is the Captain. If somebody gets left behind, they float back to the Captain in a bubble. Anyone can ask for a lift by pressing a trigger, and the Captain can press one to call the whole squad over.

The first time you play, the game asks your name. Tap one, spell it, or skip. After that it says welcome back.

## Built for a kid who is still learning to read

A voice reads every mission and everything the characters say. The game picks the most natural voice your browser has, and the pause menu lets you try the others or turn it off.

Nobody can lose. Walk to an edge and you stop there. Miss a jump and a bubble carries you straight back to where you were standing. The robot king's attacks only bounce you backwards. Rocks on the surf road only make you wobble.

{{photo /shots/galactic-king-grumble.jpg King Grumble, mid-stomp. The yellow ring is a shockwave. You jump over it.}}

## How to play

Open the game in a browser. It works on a computer, a tablet or a phone.

- **Xbox controller:** press A to join. The left stick moves. A jumps, and A again in the air is a double jump. Hold A on the way down to float. X spins, B is a rocket dash and Y says hello. The right stick looks around. The Menu button pauses.
- **Keyboard:** press Space to join. Move with WASD or the arrows, jump with Space, spin with J, dash with K and say hello with L. Q and E turn the view.
- **Phone or tablet:** turn it sideways and tap to start. A thumb stick appears wherever your left thumb lands, the buttons are on the right, and dragging the picture looks around.

The pause menu has the missions for the world you are in, a full screen switch, a photo button and the settings.

## How it was tested

Claude played it before any of us did. It ran the game in a browser with pretend controllers plugged in, then walked, jumped, flew, raced and surfed through every world, with up to three players at once. It earned two of the three stars in each of the four newest worlds that way, and tickled the king three times.

{{photo /shots/galactic-snowball-comet.jpg Snowball Comet. The penguin by the trees is lost, and the igloo is home.}}

## Where it falls short

- Every shape is built from code. There are no artist-made models, so it looks like a toy box and not like a studio game.
- Claude could not hold a real controller, hear the music or see how fast it runs on our computer. Those tests belong to River.
- The gem hunts in the four newest worlds were only partly played through, so a gem may turn out to be harder to reach than it should be.
- The touch controls were tested on a pretend phone, not in a hand. A phone is one player. Three players still need controllers.
- Sound sometimes stays off until you click the game once. Browsers do that.
- It does not rival the latest Mario. Nintendo can relax.

## About that astrophysics

The Secret Satellite has low gravity. You jump higher and come down slower. If that leads to one real question about why, I'll count the phase as on schedule.

Until then the questions are about which gate to walk into next.
