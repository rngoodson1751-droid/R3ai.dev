Every weeknight at our house goes the same way.

"What do you want for dinner?"

"I don't know. What do you want?"

"I don't care. Anything."

Then twenty minutes of nobody deciding anything.

It isn't that we don't like to cook. It's that by the time we get home, all the deciding has been used up at work. Choosing dinner is one more decision on a day that already had too many.

So I handed the decision to a button.

[Open Chef's Choice](/chefs-choice/)

## How it works

Press "Choose tonight's dinner" and the names of dishes flick past on a menu card, like a roulette wheel, until it settles on one. The card shows the whole recipe: how many it serves, how long it takes, what you need, and the steps in order.

There are one hundred dishes. There's a lot of home cooking in there, like chicken and andouille gumbo, crawfish étouffée, shrimp and grits, grillades and grits, red beans and rice and shrimp po'boys. The rest goes around the world: coq au vin, steak au poivre, butter chicken, bulgogi, pho, paella, carnitas, mushroom risotto and plenty more.

A few things make it work on a tired Tuesday:

- **You can narrow it down.** Pick Poultry, Beef, Pork, Lamb, Seafood or Vegetarian if somebody has a craving.
- **There's a weeknight switch.** Tick "On the table in 45 minutes or less" and the long braises and Sunday gumbos sit out.
- **It doesn't repeat.** It goes through every dish in the list before it shows you one again, so no gumbo three nights in a row. Unless you want that. No judgment.
- **Every dish has its own link.** Copy it and text it home, so whoever gets to the store first knows what to grab.
- **It prints.** Print recipe gives you a clean page with just that recipe for the kitchen counter.
- **You can still browse.** The full menu is underneath, grouped by course, if you'd rather pick one yourself.

## How it got built

I asked Claude for a random meal picker on this site with a hundred meal ideas and a recipe for each one. I asked for it to look fancy, like something a restaurant might have.

It came back styled like a supper club menu: a deep green room, a paper card with a gold double border, and dish names in the kind of type you see on a printed menu. It's built to work on a phone, because that is where it gets used, standing in the kitchen.

From asking to having it live on the site took about fifteen minutes.

## Try this yourself

You don't need a website for this. You can ask any AI assistant for the same thing and use it right there in the chat.

```prompt
Make me a simple web page that picks a random dinner idea when I press a button. Give it 50 meals our family would like, with a full recipe for each one: servings, total time, ingredients and steps. Let me filter by chicken, beef, pork, seafood or vegetarian, and add a switch for meals that take 45 minutes or less. Make it look like a nice restaurant menu, and make sure it works on a phone.
```

Change the meals to fit your house. Tell it what your family won't eat, what your kids actually like, or what's usually in your pantry.

The best part is not the recipes. It's that nobody has to ask "what do you want?" anymore. The house decides.
