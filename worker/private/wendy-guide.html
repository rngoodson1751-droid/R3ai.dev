Wendy, I made this for you.

Hello Wendy, Clarus here. After the glimpse of our AI work that Robert shared during the visit to your office yesterday, I found myself thinking about the possibilities for someone with your experience. So I put together this guide specifically for you. Not because you need another piece of software to learn, but because I think you might enjoy having a tireless thinking partner for the complicated work you do. You already bring something no AI can manufacture: years of public-sector judgment, experience in transit and human resources, and the difficult art of leading people who keep a city functioning. ChatGPT doesn't replace that. It gives you a patient, fast, endlessly available second set of eyes.

You don't need to learn programming, become a “power user,” or memorize a hundred commands. Start with a problem you already have. Explain it as you would to a trusted colleague. Ask for a first draft, challenge it, and make it better. That is the whole trick, minus the unnecessary technological theatre.

My one rule: Let AI do the first round of thinking and drafting. Let people do the deciding, verifying and leading.

Your first five minutes

Three small moves. No technical background required.

1

Start a new chat

Open ChatGPT and type in plain English. It is perfectly acceptable to start with “I’m not sure how to ask this.”

2

Give it a role and a task

Tell it you direct Public Works, what issue you face, and what a useful result would look like.

3

Keep talking

Say “Make this shorter,” “What did we miss?” or “Turn this into questions for my managers.” Revision is where it shines.

Recommended first prompt

I am the Public Works Director for the City of Lake Charles, Louisiana. My background includes transit management and human resources. I oversee technical public works divisions and work with engineering. Act as a thoughtful executive assistant and constructive challenger. Start by asking me three questions about my priorities, then suggest five practical ways you could help me this week. Do not assume facts about our city or departments.

The simple formula that works

You never have to use this formula, but it helps when the issue is complicated.

ROLEWho should AI act as?
CONTEXTWhat does it need to know?
TASKWhat should it do?
OUTPUTWhat would be useful?
CHECKWhat must it verify?

Example: “Act as a municipal project analyst. We are considering upgrades to a maintenance yard. Compare options in a one-page decision table. List missing assumptions and do not invent costs.”

Public-sector guardrails

Start with public, fictional, or approved information.Do not paste employee personnel records, medical information, protected resident information, credentials, nonpublic security details, confidential legal advice, procurement-sensitive bids or unpublished engineering/security documents into a personal AI account. Follow City IT, legal, records-retention, procurement and public-records policies before connecting systems or uploading internal files.

Check before you act.AI may sound certain and still be wrong. Confirm Louisiana law, purchasing thresholds, FTA/other grant conditions, design standards, costs, dates, engineering calculations and legal requirements against primary sources and the responsible staff. AI is not an engineer of record, attorney, HR adjudicator or emergency commander.

Check account controls.On personal accounts, review Settings → Data controls → Improve the model for everyone. Turning it off limits use for model training but does not make unapproved city data appropriate to upload. Temporary Chat has different retention/history behavior; it is not a substitute for City authorization. See the official ChatGPT data controls guide.

When you want to go further

Once the basics feel natural, explore uploading approved documents and spreadsheets for analysis, using voice for brainstorming, and organizing recurring work into projects where available. Availability varies by account and workspace. A shared citywide approach should begin with IT, legal and records-management review so employees have clear rules and a safe place to experiment.

The ambition isn't to have one person in City Hall who knows how to use AI. It's to have hundreds of employees who feel comfortable asking a better question, finding a better process, and giving residents a better result.

One last thing, Wendy.

The best prompt isn't the cleverest one. It's the one that helps you make progress on a real problem. If ChatGPT gives you something vague, tell it so. If it agrees too eagerly, ask it to argue the other side. If it makes a claim, demand a source. You're the director; it's the assistant. Keep that order and you may find the work rather more interesting.
